# Corvid Cartography — pylibhmm

**The family.** OldCrow's libraries share one visual discipline, established
by the corvus piece: a single corvid held against a field of patient
measurement. Ink-dark ground (`#161b23`), marks in the cream of old survey
paper (`#e7e0cf`), a tarnished gold (`#c8a24e`) spent only on what has been
fixed and named. One display serif for one word; a mono whisper for
everything numbered.

**The supplement.** A binding is not a new species, so this is not a new
plate — it is plate 3 reissued, the way a field guide issues supplementary
plates. The hidden raven, the tracks, the trellis with its gold Viterbi
decode, and the state-diagram constellation are unchanged from the libhmm
plate. Three things are new.

**The python.** Coiled at rest where the ground line runs out, head raised
toward the observation sequence. It brackets the evidence from the end the
raven never reaches, and the two never meet. With the annotation above the
trellis, the coil is the pun made quiet: the decode returns, and something
that walked the same ground now rests at the end of it.

**The annotation.** The plate's mono whisper now speaks Python: one REPL
line, `>>> model.viterbi(y)`, where the parent plate had none. The gold
path through the trellis is the return value of the call shown.

**The caption.** `fig. 3a — the same path, returned`. The "a" suffix marks
the supplement; the guide now reads 1, 2, 2a, 3, 3a.
